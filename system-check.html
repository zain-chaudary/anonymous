A full-screen demonstration of what a compromise looks like: trace, terminated connections, disabled security, a fake “factory reset” countdown. Nothing on this device will be changed — no files touched, no reset, no connections closed, and no address looked up. It is a web page pretending.

  • Press ESC at any time to end it instantly
  • Works on any device; sound is optional
  • Play it on your own screen, or with someone who is in on the joke

Ideal for a class, a security briefing, or colleagues who know a demo is running. Not for anyone anxious about losing their data, and never on a work laptop you do not own — the scariest part of a real incident is that you cannot press ESC.

That was fake. All of it.

No files were deleted, nothing was reset, no connection was closed, and no address was looked up — a web page cannot do any of that. Every number on that screen was generated locally in your browser.

“Public IP”203.0.113.47 is a reserved documentation address. It is guaranteed not to belong to anyone.
“Factory reset”There was no countdown loop underneath. It was a CSS ring and a number.
“Files destroyed”Filenames were invented. A browser cannot read your disk.
“Connections cut”A page cannot close your network. Nothing was disconnected.

Why the real thing feels different

  • No countdown. Real ransomware encrypts quietly in seconds and leaves a text file. You find out afterwards.
  • No theatrics. The dangerous ones show you nothing at all — that is why they spread.
  • You cannot press ESC. Recovery means a clean image and a backup, not closing a tab.
